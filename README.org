#+title: World Gov domain names

This repository collects the government domain names of 197 States and
entities, so we can measure how much governments contribute to
publicly available software archived by Software Heritage.

* Scope

197 jurisdictions, each tagged with its UN status: the 193 UN member
states, the 2 UN observer states (Holy See, Palestine) and 2 non-UN
entities (Taiwan, Kosovo), kept for context but excluded from any
UN-facing aggregation.

Government domains are sorted into levels:

- =central= -- the central government: ministries, national agencies
  (including national research bodies: CNRS, CNR…), public
  universities run by the State, and the root domains that stand for
  them (=gouv.fr=, =gov.uk=).
- =central-1= -- the first administrative tier below: state, Land,
  region… (=sp.gov.br=, =nouvelle-aquitaine.fr=), and the public
  universities it runs.
- =local= -- everything lower (municipalities, counties, parishes…):
  out of the central+ lists, but still a public-sector domain.

Where each level has its own roots, listing them is enough; where a
single root like =gov.br= also covers local levels, the local labels
are listed as =local= so that they leave the root.

Per country, two hand-edited files carry every decision:
=curated.csv= (the confirmed domains, with their level) and
=excluded.csv= (what is out of scope). Everything else is generated
from the official registries plus =curated.csv= minus =excluded.csv=.

* What you get

** Consolidated lists

Two ready-to-use CSV files, covering UN members and observers only:

- =data/public-sector-domains.csv= -- every government host we found,
  apex domains *and* subdomains (e.g. =gouv.fr=, =beta.gouv.fr=). Each
  row carries metadata: UN status, region, languages and GDP.
- =data/public-sector-domains-central+-policy.csv= -- the central
  government plus the first administrative tier below it, as a match
  policy table, =domain,kind,country=: =subtree= is a domain and
  everything below it (=gouv.fr=), =exact= a domain alone
  (=sp.gov.br=), =exclude= a domain and everything below it to keep
  out (=abingdon.gov.uk=). The most specific row (the longest domain)
  decides, so any regex engine or none can apply it. Every =subtree=
  and =exact= row is a confirmed domain, by an official registry or by
  hand: together they are the short list of email domains to watch.

** Per-country data (=countries/<ISO3>_<slug>/=)

One directory per country (=FRA_france=, =USA_united_states=, …), with:

- =curated.csv= -- the confirmed domains, decided by hand
  (=domain,level,name,kind=, =kind= =exact= only for a root shared
  with a lower tier). The place to add a domain.
- =excluded.csv= -- what is out of scope altogether, as full hostnames
  (=domain,reason=): rejected proposals (not a public body, a typo…).
  A domain listed here never comes back in =proposed.csv=.
- =curate_todo.csv= -- the curation worklist: the proposed domains no
  one has decided on yet, in the columns of =curated.csv=, ready to
  move there. Generated.
- =proposed.csv= -- the same domains scored 0 to 10 by how many
  sources agree, with these sources. Generated.
- =summary.md= -- a human-readable cross-check report, naming the
  country's authoritative registries if any. Generated.
- =sources/= -- the raw data behind the above, one folder per source,
  never edited by hand.

** Master data (=data/=)

- =world-governments.csv= -- the master list of the 197 countries.
- =sources/= -- the country metadata, one table per source (CIA World
  Factbook, UN/DESA, IANA, OECD, region, languages, population, GDP)
  and one row per country. Generated by =bb pipeline enrich=.
- A few generated mapping files linking country names to source IDs.

* Where the data comes from

- *crt.sh* -- subdomains seen in public TLS certificates (main harvest).
- *Wayback Machine* -- hosts captured by the Internet Archive (its CDX
  index), on demand for the roots crt.sh leaves empty.
- *HTTPS and MX probes* -- check that a host actually serves content
  and receives email. Kept out of the CSV files, in a local
  [[https://github.com/datalevin/datalevin][Datalevin]] database, =data/.db= (not versioned).
- *Wikidata* -- ministries, parliaments, central banks, courts,
  universities and research institutes, with their official websites.
- *ROR* -- the universities the [[https://ror.org][Research Organization Registry]]
  knows, as candidates to confirm.
- *Official registries and directories of public bodies* -- CISA's
  federal =.gov= list, France's =lannuaire= and =esr=, the UK's CDDO
  list, Norway's Enhetsregisteret, Germany's Behördenwegweiser… An
  authoritative directory confirms its domains directly; a mixed one
  feeds =proposed.csv= with a strong score bonus.
- *Link graph* -- how many public-sector domains of the same country
  link to a host, from [[https://github.com/mgifford/eu-plus-government-scans][eu-plus-government-scans]] (EU and
  neighbours only).
- *IANA, UN/DESA, CIA World Factbook, OECD* -- country metadata, also
  used to cross-check candidates.

[[https://gitlab.softwareheritage.org/swh/products/insights/swh-sopc-data-sources][swh-sopc-data-sources]] lists all the data sources of the final
[[https://stateofpubliccode.org/][State of Public Code]], these directories included.

* Forges and source-code catalogs

The search for government forges the [[https://archive.softwareheritage.org][Software Heritage archive]] does
not know yet, and for source-code catalogs, lives in
[[https://gitlab.softwareheritage.org/swh/products/insights/swh-sopc-data-sources][swh-sopc-data-sources]] (=bb forges=): it reads the policy table and
the harvest of this repository.

* Running the pipeline

Everything is driven by Babashka through =bb.edn= tasks.

#+begin_src bash
# Install Babashka (Linux / macOS)
bash <(curl -s https://raw.githubusercontent.com/babashka/babashka/master/install)

# From the repository root:
bb pipeline help     # list all sub-commands
bb pipeline all      # full run: collect + enrich + build + report
#+end_src

See =scripts/README.org= for the full command reference, scoring rules
and environment variables.
